ROOT CAUSES
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 2
Why These Gaps Exist
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 5
Underrepresented in the data
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 7
Female patients make up 21% of the dataset vs. 33% real-world CHD prevalence (BHF, 2021). The model learns less about this group — and recalls it less reliably.
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 10
No review mechanisms by design
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 12
The original pipeline had no clinician override, patient challenge process, audit trail, or version history built in from the start.

MITIGATION APPROACH
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 2
Two Targeted Interventions
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 6
1
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 7
Fairness-Aware Training
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 9
ADDRESSES BIAS SUPPRESSION
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 10
An ExponentiatedGradient algorithm, constrained by Fairlearn's EqualizedOdds criterion (tolerance 0.01), re-weighted the training data so error rates moved closer together across male and female patients.
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 14
2
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 15
Confidence-Based Flagging
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 17
ADDRESSES CORRECTABILITY
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 18
Predictions with model confidence between 30% and 70% are automatically flagged for clinician review — adding a human check on the cases the model is least sure about.

CONCLUSION
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 2
Fairness Requires More Than a Better Model
--- shape: TEXT_BOX (17) TextBox 3
Three of six procedural fairness criteria passed at baseline. Mitigation narrowed the gender recall gap and strengthened oversight — at a modest accuracy cost — without fully resolving either. The priority that matters most now is correctability: the model's weakest technical result, and the public's strongest expectation.
